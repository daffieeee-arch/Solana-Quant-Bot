# Whale tracker step 0: bundles and payer clusters from existing columns, 2026-10-11

First result for roadmap #167 (plan §8, step 0): clusters of wallets that buy the same token in the same slot, found in columns the store already has (`fee_payer`, `jito_tip`, `tx_index`, several buyers in one transaction). Two questions: how fast do these clusters sell, and what does a veto on them do to F7 and its random control N2, net of costs.

**Sample: two UTC days (2026-09-25 and 2026-09-26, slots 450,184,450–450,829,355), development data only.** Everything below is a first look on 2 of the 18.7 development days; the full run is proposed at the end. Code: `lab/wallets/clusters.py`, `lab/wallets/veto.py`, `lab/wallets/report_step0.py` (tests in `lab/wallets/tests/`). Output, outside Git: `data-old-faithful-one/lab/wallets/clusters-v1-sample/` and `veto-v1-sample/`.

## Method

A **cluster event** is ≥ 2 different wallets buying the same token (bonding curve, or one PumpSwap pool) in the same slot, linked by at least one of:

| link | meaning |
|---|---|
| `tx` | the buys are in one transaction (every buying wallet co-signs it) |
| `pay` | the same fee payer, when it is not the buying wallet itself; the payer is a node of the cluster |
| `tip` | adjacent transactions (`tx_index` differs by 1) with the same Jito tip > 0: a likely bundle |

Links are joined with union-find per (token, slot). Fee payers that paid for ≥ 100 wallets in ≥ 50 tokens in the previous 24 h are services (gas sponsors, relayers) and give no `pay` link; that registry only looks back. On the sample day 18 payers met this and paid 843k trades; ~400 payers with 20–49 wallets in a median of 4 tokens look like bundlers and stay clusters.

For every cluster event and for every solo buy (baseline) we measure the share of the bought tokens that the event's wallets sold of that token (curve and pools of the mint together) within 3 s, 40 s and 10 min (milliseconds → slots per epoch). Sells in the same slot count only after the event's last buy.

**Veto variants** (fixed before any outcome was read; t = decision slot, windows include slot t itself because whole slots ≤ t are known at the decision):

- **K1**: a cluster event on the same token in [t − 60 s, t];
- **K2**: a cluster event on the same token in [t − 10 min, t];
- **K3**: the triggering seller was a member of a cluster on this token in [t − 24 h, t) (F7 only; N2 has no trigger seller).

A vetoed position counts as 0 P&L (same convention as the O7 overlay in `backtest/report.py`), so Δ = −(sum of the vetoed positions' P&L) / positions. Positions and P&L come from the existing tournament runs, base costs: `f7rev-1033-1046` (engine 59ca18c, F7 reversed pools and N2, sizes 0.5/1/2 SOL) and `v1.1-dev-1040-1046` (engine 60c415f, F7 curve / normal pools / reversed pools and N2, 0.5 SOL). Day bootstrap CIs are computed but with 2 days they say nothing; the table shows the two per-day values instead.

## 1. How many clusters, and how fast do they sell

145 windows of 20 min. 17,815 cluster events (12,733 wallets, 6,719 tokens) against 27.4M solo buys: 0.07% of buy events and 0.4% of the SOL (89k of 22.8M SOL; the reversed-pool farming volume dominates the denominator). Shares are by event count, so high-frequency bots dominate the solo rows.

| kind | venue | create slot | link | events | SOL bought | sold ≤3 s | ≤40 s | ≤10 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cluster | curve | no | pay | 495 | 360 | 4% | 32% | 57% |
| cluster | curve | no | tip | 4,553 | 2,584 | 5% | 31% | 92% |
| cluster | curve | no | tx | 808 | 678 | 2% | 33% | 96% |
| solo | curve | no | | 2,989,257 | 1,045,655 | 7% | 42% | 85% |
| cluster | curve | **yes** | tx | 5,746 | 29,079 | 16% | 69% | 91% |
| solo | curve | **yes** | | 119,756 | 240,366 | 28% | 70% | 88% |
| cluster | pool normal | no | pay | 880 | 1,554 | 21% | 47% | 67% |
| cluster | pool normal | no | tip | 2,719 | 2,117 | 1% | 19% | 72% |
| cluster | pool normal | no | **tx** | 2,399 | **49,914** | 0% | 6% | **23%** |
| solo | pool normal | no | | 12,421,419 | 3,968,397 | 5% | 23% | 57% |

(Rows with < 30 events are left out: curve create-slot `pay`/`tip`, reversed `pay`/`tip`.)

SOL-weighted, per venue: curve outside the create slot 47% sold within 40 s and 90% within 10 min; curve create slot 39% / 83%; normal pools 0.2% / 25%; reversed pools 97% / 99.5%.

What this says:

- **Clusters on the curve are flippers like everyone else.** Outside the create slot they sell 90–96% of their tokens within 10 min, the same as solo buyers (85%). Tip-bundles (4,553 events, 0.2 SOL median) are the most common form.
- **Create-slot bundles are the sniper structure the plan described.** 5,746 transactions with 2–14 buying wallets (median 3.5 SOL, p90 6.7 SOL): 69% sold within 40 s. They hold 29k SOL in two days, but they are never tradable for us (we do not enter in the create slot), so they matter only as a label for step 1.
- **One structure holds: multi-wallet buys in one PumpSwap transaction.** 2,399 events, 49.9k SOL (p90 19.7 SOL per event, ≤ 6 wallets, one payer per transaction): 6% sold within 40 s, 23% within 10 min. This is the only cluster type that looks like accumulation instead of flipping. It is the candidate for step 1 labels ("split buyer") and for the watchlist.
- **Reversed-pool `tx` clusters are a 2-wallet pair** (184 events, always 20 SOL, 97% sold within 10 min): a farming pattern, not a whale.

- **On organic reversed pools the veto hurts, on both days, at every size and delay.** The positions that K1 or K2 would skip were on average *better* than the rest: K1 removes 3.8% of positions and costs 0.0005 SOL per position (≈ 17% of the candidate's +0.0028 net at 0.5 SOL, d = 1). A cluster buy shortly before an F7 trigger in these pools is apparently part of the inflow that F7 wants, not a warning sign.
- K2 helps only in **flagged** (farming) pools, which do not count for the gate. In flagged pools no cluster ever falls within 60 s before a trigger (K1 = 0%): the clusters there are the 2-wallet farming pairs, far from the triggers.
- K3 (the trigger seller was in a cluster on this token in the last 24 h) fires on 4% and changes nothing (one day of data).

- On the curve a cluster veto improves F7 (+0.0076 at K2) but improves the random control **more** (+0.0093). Tokens with a recent cluster are bad for everybody; the veto removes exposure, it does not add edge. F7's edge over N2 shrinks from +0.0081 to +0.0064, and both stay far below zero (curve F7 is STOP in v1.1).
- In normal pools K2 vetoes 22% of F7 positions and improves them by +0.0026 against +0.0008 for N2, but F7 stays below N2 (−0.0202 vs −0.0187).

### Graduations in the create slot, reported apart

Tokens that graduated in their own create slot are 33% of the F7 positions in normal pools in the sample (11,031 of 33,553) and the worst segment for everyone: F7 −0.0469 per position against −0.0109 on the other tokens; N2 −0.0567 against −0.0132. The cluster veto changes little there (K2: 13% vetoed, +0.0028). The graduation itself is known at decision time, so "graduated in the create slot" is a cheap filter for pool strategies on young pools, separate from this veto; it is an observation on two days, not a tested variant. On the curve such tokens have no tradable trigger (no curve trades after the create slot), and in reversed pools the run has none.

## Conclusion of step 0 (two days)

1. Clusters from the existing columns are rare (0.07% of buys) and, apart from one structure, flip as fast as everybody else. The structure that holds is the multi-wallet buy in one PumpSwap transaction (50k SOL in two days, 23% sold within 10 min).
2. **A cluster veto does not help the live candidate F7-reversed.** On organic pools it costs 0.0003–0.0009 SOL per position at the gate sizes; the gains on the curve and in normal pools go to N2 as much or more, so they are exposure, not edge. There is no basis to add K1–K3 as an overlay.
3. Create-slot bundles (sniping) and the split buyers are the labels that step 1 (the wallet score) needs; the clusters table is the input for that.

## Limitations

- Two days, one regime (267 ms), no regime split; CIs are not meaningful at 2 days. K3 has one day because of its 24 h look-back.
- Transfers are invisible: a wallet that funds another wallet and does not pay its fees is not linked. The `signers` column exists only in the raw chunks (epoch ≥ 1043); the `tx` link covers the buying co-signers, not a wallet that only co-signs.
- The service-payer rule (≥ 100 wallets and ≥ 50 tokens in 24 h) is a judgment call; the boundary cases (payers with 20–49 wallets) are kept as clusters.
- Positions come from the tournament runs at their engine commits, not from a fresh replay.

## Proposed next run (full development period)

The two sample days ran as two single-day processes: ~5 min each, DuckDB at 1 GB, peak RSS 1.2 GB, 2 threads, `nice 10`, output 6.7 MB. The full development period (epochs 1033–1046, 18.7 days) is ~90 min at the same footprint, so a heavy job (> 10 min, > 1 GB) under `heavy.lock` after the families-v2 run, or on the NAS:

```
# commit: this PR's head; needs the dev2 store read-only (42 GB) and week2-defaults.yaml
python -m wallets.clusters --from-slot 446256000 --to-slot 452301700 --out $LAB_WALLETS_OUT/clusters-v1
python -m wallets.veto --run <backtests>/f7rev-1033-1046 --clusters $LAB_WALLETS_OUT/clusters-v1 \
    --from-slot 446256000 --to-slot 452301700 --out $LAB_WALLETS_OUT/veto-v1   # ~2 min
```

(`--to-slot` keeps the 10-min sell tail before the hold-out; `LAB_STORE`, `LAB_DEFAULTS` and `LAB_WALLETS_OUT` override the VPS paths.) Expected: ~90 min, 1.3 GB RAM, 2 cores, ~70 MB output. Then the same tables over 18 days with day-bootstrap CIs, split by regime (316 ms vs 267 ms).
